# Loose Footed Spritsails

The loose footed spritsail consists of a trapazoidal sail with the luff edge
attached to the mast. There is a round wooden sprit that levers up from where it
is attached on the mast to the peak along the head of the sailcloth. The device
that attaches the sprit to the mast is called a snotter. The Loose Footed
Spritsail does not have a boom and the foot is "loose", somewhat like a lanteen.

The Spritsail is found in the Other section of the shipyard.

The texture handling is the same as the fisherman staysails

## Fitting

The Loose Footed Spritsail can be attached to any mast on any boat. The starting
size is simmilar to the Full Gaff. The inside edge of the snotter is flush with
the mast and the rotation plate rests under the snotter. The spirt is mounted to
the sprit mount rod which rotates with the snotter around the mast as the
direction of the wind requires. This rotation is the same physics as on a gaff
sail where the boom rotates around the mast with the foot of the sail.

## Controls

### Sheeting

The clew of the Loose Footed Spritsail is sheeted on either or both a port winch
and a starboard winch, depending on the tack. Both control lines attach to the
clew, terminating there.

### Reefing or Brailing

To Reef a spritsail, the halyard can be wound up to pull the sprit in a more
parallel angle to the mast. A rope attaches to the top of the sprit and routs to
the pully on the mast, similar to where a gaff sail would have a line that
attaches to the gaff spar to the to pully at the top of the mast. The bottom
portion of the luff edge of the sail should only be secured to the mast in a few
places so that the cloth can gather towards the snotter during reefing.

## Geometry

The loose footed spritsail billows from the clew through an arch to the mast.
The cloth around the clew is curved towards the boat.

## Statistics

The loose footed spritsail is cheeper than most sails with a cost similar to the
Junk sail.

It has less force than the gaff sail but more than the Junk Sail.
